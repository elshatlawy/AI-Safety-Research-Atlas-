AI Safety Research Atlas · First test · run 24 to 31 October 2026

Does the atlas make answers better?

Side by side, answers written with the atlas were better in 38 of 52 questions and worse in 8, mainly because they held about half as many mistakes. This is a first test, judged by AI; no person has checked the verdicts yet.

How it was run

  1. An AI answered 52 open research questions twice: once with the web only, once with the web and the atlas.
  2. Each answer had at most 250 words, cited arXiv papers for every claim, and did not mention the atlas.
  3. Two AI judges compared the two answers to each question without knowing which used the atlas, and picked the better one: accuracy first, then coverage, then whether it states assumptions and open parts.
  4. The second judge saw the answers in the opposite order. A side wins a question only when both judges pick it.
  5. Two more AI graders also scored each answer on its own, checking every cited paper.

Graded one answer at a time

Before the side-by-side test, two AI graders checked every cited paper in each answer on its own. Both sides scored near the top, so this could not tell a good answer from a better one; that is why the side-by-side test was added. Without the atlas, answers cited more correct papers.

Limits

  • All judging and grading is by AI, from the same model family that wrote the answers. No person has checked the verdicts yet.
  • The questions and the reference papers come from the atlas, which may favour the atlas side.
  • One setting only: a strong AI model with web access. A closed-book run, a weaker model or a human reader may differ.
  • Each answer was written once.
