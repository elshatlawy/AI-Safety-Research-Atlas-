What the atlas is

A map of AI-safety research that you can check. Every record quotes its source exactly, every link says why it exists, and nothing counts until it passes checks built to catch planted mistakes.

Approaches that cut across areas

Pick one to see the problems it serves; they light up on the map above.

Debate, covered. Two AIs argue opposite sides so that a weaker judge can find the truth. Used on Scalable oversight (Alignment), Judging AI outputs (Human factors), Safety cases (Assurance and verification) and Factual errors and hallucination (Robustness).

Amplification and decomposition, covered. Split a hard task into pieces a person can check, then build the answer back up. Used on Scalable oversight (Alignment).

AI critiques and AI feedback, covered. AI models point out flaws or give feedback in place of human reviewers. Used on Scalable oversight (Alignment) and Learning from human feedback (Alignment).

Prover-verifier games, covered. Train a model to give answers that a weaker checker can verify. Used on Scalable oversight (Alignment) and Judging AI outputs (Human factors).

Weak-to-strong training, covered. Train a strong model with labels from a weaker supervisor and measure what carries over. Used on Scalable oversight (Alignment).

Oversight experiments and benchmarks, covered. Studies and benchmarks that test whether an oversight method works. Used on Scalable oversight (Alignment).

RLHF and reward models, partly covered. Train a reward model on human preferences, then train the AI to score well on it. Used on Learning from human feedback (Alignment), Specifying goals and rewards (Alignment) and Persuasion and manipulation (Human factors).

Training on written principles, partly covered. Train models or safeguards against a written list of rules. Used on Learning from human feedback (Alignment) and Jailbreaks (Robustness).

Model organisms, partly covered. Deliberately build misaligned models to study a risk safely. Used on Deceptive alignment and scheming (Alignment), Emergent misalignment (Alignment), Backdoors (Security), Auditing for hidden objectives (Interpretability) and Propensity evaluations (Evaluation and risk assessment).

Probing and representation reading, partly covered. Read concepts, such as what the model takes to be true, directly from its internal activity. Used on Finding the concepts a model uses (Interpretability) and Honesty and latent knowledge (Alignment).

Sparse autoencoders, not touched yet. Break a model's internal activity into many sparse, readable features. Used on Finding the concepts a model uses (Interpretability) and Reverse-engineering circuits (Interpretability).

Red teaming, partly covered. People or AIs attack a system on purpose to find its failures. Used on Jailbreaks (Robustness), Control protocols (Control and monitoring), Preventing misuse (Security) and Dangerous capabilities (Evaluation and risk assessment).

AI monitors, partly covered. One model watches another's actions or reasoning and flags danger. Used on Control protocols (Control and monitoring), Untrusted monitoring and collusion (Control and monitoring), Monitoring reasoning (Control and monitoring) and Safe agent design (Control and monitoring).

Machine-checked proofs, partly covered. Proofs checked line by line by the Lean proof assistant. Used on Proofs about safety protocols (Assurance and verification), Limits of monitoring (Control and monitoring), Scalable oversight (Alignment) and Guaranteed-safe designs (Assurance and verification).

Information theory, partly covered. Measuring how much a judge or monitor can learn from what it sees. Used on Limits of monitoring (Control and monitoring) and Scalable oversight (Alignment).

Singular learning theory, partly covered. Track what a model learns through the shape of its loss landscape. Used on How training shapes models (Foundations and theory) and Reverse-engineering circuits (Interpretability).

Cryptography, partly covered. Use cryptography to hide, or to prove, properties of a model. Used on Backdoors (Security) and Limits of verification (Foundations and theory).

Game theory, partly covered. Model AI interactions as games with incentives. Used on Scalable oversight (Alignment) and Cooperation and conflict (Multi-agent risks).

Activation steering, partly covered. Change a model's behaviour by editing its internal activity directly. Used on Finding the concepts a model uses (Interpretability) and Auditing for hidden objectives (Interpretability).

Pretraining data curation, not touched yet. Filter what a model learns from in the first place, for example to leave out weapons knowledge. Used on Preventing misuse (Security), Tamper-resistant safeguards (Robustness) and Dangerous capabilities (Evaluation and risk assessment).
