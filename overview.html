What it found

The four results that matter most from the debate pilot. All are AI drafts that passed two blind AI checks; none is confirmed by an expert.

DebateTwo AI systems argue opposite answers and a judge, a person or a weaker AI, decides. The hope: honesty wins, so a weak judge can oversee a strong AI.
Safety caseA structured argument, broken into steps, that a system is safe enough to deploy. Every step needs evidence.
LeanA programming language for maths. A computer checks every step of a proof written in it.
The debate safety case

2 of 45 steps have checked support

Buhl et al. 2025 sketched a safety case for debate in 45 steps: the top claim, 22 supporting claims, 4 assumptions, 12 ways the argument could fail, 4 open problems and 2 kinds of evidence it still needs. We matched every step against the results in the 42 papers.

Supported: “Efficient equilibrium debaters exist” and “Protocol avoids obfuscated arguments”, both backed by Brown-Cohen et al. 2025. Contested: “Human judges have bounded systematic error”.

Does debate help the judge?

Debate beats consultancy in 51 of 56 matched comparisons

In consultancy a single AI argues for one assigned answer; in debate two argue both sides. Each dot is one matched comparison on the same task: how many points more often the judge picked the right answer with debate.

other 4 papersKenton et al. 2024 (33)points of judge accuracy

The median gain is +8.4 points across 5 papers, but 33 of the 56 comparisons come from one paper and 45 use LLM judges.

Questions the papers' authors left open, worked once by the pilot. Narrowed means a smaller version is now settled and what remains is stated; partly answered means later papers settle part of it.

The finding that cuts across all four: the five assumptions the papers rely on most appear in no step of the safety case. The safety case leans on "training reaches equilibrium" (15 steps), which only 6 results address.

The best next steps

Credibility first, because it multiplies everything else. A new area built on unconfirmed checks would inherit the same doubt.

1

Get the finds confirmed

Experts check the 9 errors from short notes: the claim, the Lean counterexample, a suggested fix. People review only a small core of debate statements, each shown beside a blind plain-maths read-back of its Lean version; AI checks cover the rest. That turns the first results into findings people can cite.

2

Measure usefulness automatically

An agent answers the 52 authors' questions with and without the atlas. We report accuracy, sources found and time taken. No people needed, and it gives the first real answer to "does this help?".

3

Make the results understandable

Understanding cards for the results the safety case leans on, and a blocker label on every open question so each one gets the right kind of agent.

4

Run the first stress test

Loosen "training reaches equilibrium" and find which of the 15 safety-case steps that rest on it still hold. Only 6 results address it today.

5

Then open the next area

Make debate a living area (below), and run the same pipeline on AI control and monitoring.

2

Stress-test one assumption: what still holds if training falls short?

The safety case assumes training reaches an equilibrium, a point where neither debater can do better by changing strategy. 15 of its steps rest on that, and only 6 results in 4 papers address it.
