1The question

Take one real safety argument: a case published in 2025 that an AI trained with debate will not sabotage research. It has 45 steps; the squares are its 4 stated assumptions.

14 of the 45 steps have nothing under them: nothing proved, nothing measured. In effect, they are assumed.

Under the others sit 67 findings from 24 papers: 10 proved, 40 measured and 17 that are limits or only argued. Most links are weak: the findings are about today’s debaters, not a trained, deployed system.

2Why it’s hard

Each of those lines is a citation in prose. A citation says a paper was used, not which claim, under which conditions, or how far it was checked.

Checking one link takes hours of reading, so dropped conditions and errors pass straight through to the decision at the top.

3What we’re building

So we are building one database where every claim keeps its exact quote from a pinned source, its conditions, what it rests on, what rests on it, and how far it has been checked.

Tools build on that one database. 15 of 24 work today; the dashed ones are still to build.

4What’s in it

Today it holds 245 sources, each pinned to an exact version, 1,224 records, each one quoted statement, and 2,000 links, each saying why it holds.

It is a wide map with one deep area. Of 83 research problems in AI safety, 1 is covered in depth, scalable oversight, with 42 papers read in full; 29 are partly covered and 53 not at all.

5What it found

In that deep area, it found:

  • 9 errors among 52 problems found by restating the theorems of 5 debate papers in Lean, a proof checker.
  • 8 conflicts results contradicted by another paper; 127 of 224 results were tested only once, and 23 hold up in a second paper.
  • 10 definitions of “debate” across 9 papers, differing on who argues, how turns work and who judges.
  • 15 steps, 6 results for “training reaches an equilibrium”, the assumption the case leans on most; “the judge is weaker”, used by 40 results, carries no step.
  • 28 of 33 open questions the authors left need an experiment to move; 7 need a proof.
  • 51 of 56 matched experiments favour debate over a single arguing AI, by 8.4 points at the median; 33 come from one paper.

Take the assumption the case leans on most: training reaches an equilibrium. 15 steps rest on it. What if training only reaches a local one, where no small change helps either debater?

Then 3 steps still stand if two checks pass, 1 stands, 5 fail, 3 are no longer needed and 3 stay open.

That the two checks are enough is proved in Lean. Among 6,207,875 small games there was no counterexample, but 338,000 places where training gets stuck on a wrong answer. The stress test →

6Does it help

Does it help? The same AI answered 52 open questions twice, with and without the atlas, and two blind AI judges compared each pair.

With the atlas, answers were better on 38, worse on 8 and split on 6, with 57 mistakes against 117.

But answers with the atlas cited fewer correct sources, 7.0 against 8.9: 138 well-graded papers are missing.

More accurate, not yet wider.

How the test was run →

So next, new papers come in automatically: find, catalog, extract, check, link, update. Every stage runs today, but by hand. Next they run on every new paper and lab post, starting with those 138.
